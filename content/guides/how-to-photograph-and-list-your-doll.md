On a pre-owned marketplace, **your listing is the product**. Buyers can't touch the doll, test the joints or look for tears. All they have is your photos and your words. A good listing doesn't need to be fancy. It needs to be clear, honest and complete.

## Step 1: get the light right

- **Use daylight, not flash.** Flash flattens skin texture and makes TPE look shiny and plasticky.
- **Stand near a window with indirect light.** Overcast days are ideal, because the light is soft and even, without harsh shadows.
- **Use a plain background**, such as a neutral wall or a clean bedsheet. Clear away clutter, other people and anything that could identify you or where you live.

## Step 2: the photos every listing needs

1. **Full body, front.** The whole doll in the frame, standing or lying flat, with nothing cropped.
2. **Full body, back.** Buyers check the back and buttocks for flat spots and wear.
3. **Face close-up**, in sharp focus, showing the makeup, eyes, lips and hairline or wig.
4. **Joint close-ups**: shoulders, elbows, hips and knees. This is where buyers look for tears and looseness.
5. **Hands and feet.** Fingers are a common place for tears.
6. **Every flaw.** Photograph each mark, tear or repair close up, next to a coin if it helps show the size.
7. **Extras**: the outfits, wigs, spare head, stand, cleaning kit and box all together.

Keep it tasteful: the doll clothed, or posed so the photos aren't explicit. That's our listing standard, and it's also what lets listings show up properly in search.

## Step 3: write the facts, not the fantasy

Buyers search by specifics and skip flowery descriptions. Include:

- **Brand and model** (including the head number, if you know it). Collectors search by these.
- **Material:** TPE, S-TPE, silicone or hybrid. If you're not sure, say so.
- **Exact height and weight**, measured rather than guessed.
- **Skeleton:** standing feet or not, and any upgrades such as articulated fingers or shrugging shoulders.
- **Ownership:** how long you've had her and roughly how often she's been used.
- **Care:** how she's been cleaned, powdered and stored.
- **Repairs:** what was repaired, when and by whom.
- **Insert:** removable or fixed, and whether a new, sealed insert is included.
- **What's included**, as a full list.

## Step 4: be honest about condition

Use a standard scale so buyers know what you mean:

- **Like new:** barely used, no marks
- **Excellent:** light use, no visible flaws
- **Very good:** minor marks, shown in the photos
- **Good:** visible wear or a professional repair
- **Fair:** significant wear, priced to match

"Slight looseness in the left hip, see photo 7" sells better than "great condition". It shows you know your doll and have nothing to hide.

## Step 5: price it realistically

Check our [pricing guide](/guides/how-much-is-my-used-doll-worth) and set your price against the condition you've described. When we review your listing, we'll suggest a price too.

## Step 6: reply quickly and directly

Most enquiries come in the first couple of days after a listing goes live. Answer questions about joints, material and flaws straight away, and send an extra photo if you're asked for one. On a brokered sale, we handle the enquiries for you.

## Copy-and-paste listing template

> **[Brand] [Model], [height] cm, [material]**
> Owned for [X months]. Used [rarely / occasionally / regularly].
> Condition: [grade]. [Describe any flaws and which photos show them.]
> Care: cleaned after every use, powdered [weekly / monthly], stored [hanging / flat] in a cool, dry room. Smoke-free home.
> Insert: [removable, with a new sealed insert included / fixed, deep-cleaned].
> Repairs: [none / details].
> Included: [wig, outfits, standing feet, spare head, box, cleaning kit…].
> Reason for selling: [optional, e.g. moving house].

## Your privacy

Keep your face, other people, post, number plates and anything that could identify your address out of every photo. On SellMyDoll your name, email and exact location are never shown, and buyers contact you through us.
