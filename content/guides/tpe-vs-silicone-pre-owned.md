When you buy new, the TPE vs silicone question is mostly about feel and budget. When you buy used, it becomes a question of **how the material ages**. A doll's material decides how she'll have been affected by her first owner's care routine (or lack of one), and how easy she'll be for you to clean and keep in good condition.

## The short version

| | TPE | Silicone |
|---|---|---|
| Feel | Softer, more "squishy" and flexible | Firmer, smoother, holds its shape |
| Typical new price | Lower | Higher, often double |
| Porous? | Yes | No |
| Ease of sanitising | Harder, can't be fully sterilised | Easier, can be cleaned thoroughly |
| Tear resistance | Lower, especially at joints | Higher |
| Ageing problems | Oiliness, stickiness, dye stains, tears | Paint wear, occasional seam splits |
| Repairs | Easier to do at home | Harder, often needs a professional |
| Best second-hand buy if… | Well powdered, removable insert, no tears | Most condition grades |

## TPE: great value, but check it carefully

TPE (thermoplastic elastomer) is what most mid-range dolls are made from, because it's soft, realistic to the touch and relatively cheap to produce. Those same properties shape how it ages:

- **It weeps oil.** TPE slowly releases plasticiser oil, which leaves the surface tacky. Regular powdering keeps this under control. A doll that hasn't been powdered for months will feel sticky and attract lint.
- **It tears where it stretches.** The armpits, inner thighs, groin, fingers and neck seam see the most stress. Small tears can be repaired with a TPE repair kit, but they tend to come back if the area keeps being stretched.
- **It takes on colour.** Dark or cheap clothing, newsprint and some fabrics leave dye marks that usually can't be removed.
- **It's porous.** This is the big one for second-hand buyers. TPE can hold moisture and bacteria below the surface, so a removable, replaceable insert matters a great deal (see our [buying guide](/guides/buying-a-second-hand-doll-uk)).

### Questions to ask a TPE seller

1. When was she last powdered, and what with?
2. Are there any tears, sticky patches or stains? Can you photograph the armpits, thighs and fingers?
3. Is the insert removable? If so, will a new, sealed one be included?
4. How has she been stored: hanging, lying flat or boxed? Has she been kept somewhere warm?
5. Has she ever been repaired?

A seller who answers all five clearly and promptly has almost certainly looked after the doll.

## Silicone: the safer pre-owned bet

Silicone dolls cost more new, which is exactly why they're attractive second-hand. You get a premium material at a mid-range price. Silicone is:

- **Non-porous**, so it can be cleaned and sanitised properly
- **More tear-resistant**, so joint areas hold up better over years of use
- **Better at holding fine detail**, such as skin texture, veins and facial sculpting
- **More heat-tolerant**, which is useful if the doll has a built-in heating system

### What to watch for on silicone

- **Paint and makeup wear** on the face, lips and nails. It's cosmetic, but repainting costs money.
- **Seam splits** where parts are bonded. These are rare, but check the neck and the joins on hybrid dolls.
- **Lubricant damage.** Silicone-based lubricant can damage a silicone doll's surface. Ask what the seller used.

## Hybrids: silicone head, TPE body

Many popular dolls pair a hand-finished silicone head with a softer TPE body. Second-hand, you get the realistic face of a premium doll at closer to a TPE price, but **the body follows TPE rules**. Check it as carefully as you would a full TPE doll.

## What about S-TPE?

S-TPE (often marketed as "super TPE") is an improved TPE blend. Manufacturers describe it as more tear-resistant and less oily. If a listing says S-TPE, that's a point in its favour, but care and cleaning still work exactly as they do for TPE.

## Lubricants and products: a quick safety note

- **Water-based lubricant** is safe for both materials.
- **Silicone-based lubricant** shouldn't be used on silicone dolls.
- **Oil-based products** (including baby oil and some makeup removers) can damage TPE.
- Use a **talc-free renewal powder** or cornflour to keep TPE dry and smooth.

## So which should you buy?

- **On a tighter budget, and happy to inspect carefully?** Choose a well-kept TPE doll with a removable insert and no tears.
- **Want the lowest-risk pre-owned purchase?** Choose silicone. It costs more up front but holds its condition much better.
- **Want a realistic face for less?** A hybrid is a good middle ground. Just check the body as you would any TPE doll.
