"What's she worth?" is usually the first question when someone thinks about selling, and the honest answer is that it depends. It depends less on what you paid than on what a buyer can confidently see in your photos.

This guide gives you a realistic starting point.

## The quick rule of thumb

| Condition | What it means | Typical share of new price |
|---|---|---|
| **Like new** | Barely used, no marks, original box | 55–70% |
| **Excellent** | Light use, no visible flaws | 45–60% |
| **Very good** | Minor marks, all disclosed and photographed | 38–50% |
| **Good** | Visible wear or a professional repair | 28–40% |
| **Fair** | Significant wear, priced to match | 15–28% |

So a TPE doll that cost £1,450 new, in excellent condition, would typically sell for around **£650–£870**. The same doll in good condition might sell for around **£400–£580**.

These ranges are a guide to the UK pre-owned market, not a guarantee. Use them as a starting point, then adjust using the factors below.

## What pushes the price up

- **Silicone or a silicone head.** Buyers trust it second-hand and pay more for it.
- **A recognised brand and model**, so buyers can check the original specification and price.
- **A removable insert, with a new, sealed one included.** This is a big trust signal on TPE dolls.
- **Extras:** spare wigs, outfits, standing feet, a second head, a hanging kit, the original box and the cleaning kit.
- **A documented care routine.** "Powdered weekly, stored hanging, smoke-free home" adds real value.
- **Great photos**, taken in daylight, showing every angle and every flaw.

## What pulls the price down

- **Tears**, especially at the armpits, groin or fingers
- **Dye stains**, which are almost always permanent on TPE
- **Loose joints**, or a skeleton that won't hold a pose
- **Strong smells**, such as smoke or damp
- **Worn makeup** on the face
- **A missing or used insert** on a TPE doll
- **Unclear or no brand information**

## How long have you had her?

Age matters less than condition. A three-year-old silicone doll that has been looked after can be worth more than a one-year-old TPE doll that's been stored badly. But buyers will ask, so be honest. A clear timeline of how long you've had her and how often she's been used builds trust.

## Pricing to sell vs pricing to wait

- **Price at the top of the range** if she's genuinely immaculate and you're happy to wait a few weeks.
- **Price in the middle** for a steady sale within a month or so.
- **Price at the bottom** if there are flaws, or you need her gone quickly (for example, before a house move).

A sensible price beats a hopeful one. A listing that has sat unsold for two months makes buyers wonder what's wrong with it.

## Parts and partial dolls

- **Spare heads** sell well, because owners like to swap looks.
- **Torsos** have their own market among people with limited space.
- **Damaged bodies** with a good head can be worth splitting, so list the head on its own.
