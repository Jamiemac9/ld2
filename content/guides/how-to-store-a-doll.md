Storage is the thing new owners worry about most, and the place most second-hand dolls pick up their damage. The good news is you don't need anything elaborate. You need a dry doll, a sensible position and the right place to keep her.

## Before she goes anywhere: dry, powdered, undressed or lightly dressed

- **Completely dry.** Any moisture left in internal areas can lead to mould. Use drying sticks until they come out dry (see our [cleaning guide](/guides/how-to-clean-a-pre-owned-doll)).
- **Lightly powdered** if she's TPE, to stop the skin sticking to itself or to fabric.
- **Undressed, or in white or light clothing.** Dark and cheap dyes transfer into TPE over time, and the marks are usually permanent.

## Your storage options

### 1. Hanging (the best option for most owners)

A hanging kit lets her hang upright from a neck hook or a shoulder bolt. Nothing presses on her body, so her shape is preserved. That matters most for the bust, hips and buttocks, where flat spots form.

- Use a kit designed for your doll's weight and skeleton, ideally from the manufacturer.
- Make sure her feet don't take her weight against the floor.
- Hang her somewhere she won't knock against a wall or door.

### 2. Lying flat

Fine for shorter spells. Lay her on her back on a soft, padded surface such as a duvet or memory foam. **Don't** lay her on a hard floor or a wooden shelf, and change her position every week or two so pressure doesn't build up in one place.

### 3. A storage case or box

Discreet, which is ideal if you share your space, but the most likely to cause pressure marks. If you use a case, make sure it's padded and long enough that she isn't bent, and take her out regularly.

### 4. Head stands

If you store the head separately, a padded head stand keeps the face away from surfaces and protects the neck connection. Cover it loosely to keep dust off.

## Looking after the skeleton

Most modern dolls have a metal skeleton with articulated joints. A little loosening over time is normal, but you can slow it right down:

- **Never force a joint.** If it resists, stop. Forcing past the natural range is how skin tears and joints fail.
- **Don't hold extreme poses for long.** Arms above the head and legs spread wide put a lot of stress on joints and on the skin over them.
- **Support her weight.** When lifting, support her under the back and knees rather than pulling by an arm or the neck.
- **Rotate positions** so no single joint is always loaded.
- **Use standing feet briefly and supervised.** They're for photos and dressing, not storage.

## Where to keep her

Look for somewhere **cool, dry and dark**:

- ✅ A wardrobe, a spare room or a cupboard on an inside wall
- ❌ Lofts and garages, where heat, cold and damp all cause problems
- ❌ Next to radiators or in direct sunlight, which yellow and soften TPE
- ❌ Touching another doll. Different materials, and different TPE skin tones, can mark each other.

## If you're planning to sell

How she's been stored is one of the first things a buyer will ask about. "Stored hanging in a cool, dry wardrobe since day one" is worth real money in a listing.
