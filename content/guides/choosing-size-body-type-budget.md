Choosing a doll is personal, and there's no wrong answer when it comes to taste. But when you're buying second-hand, it pays to think about **how she'll fit into your actual life** as well as how she looks: how you'll lift her, clean her and store her, and how her build affects wear.

## Weight: the factor people underestimate

Most full-size dolls weigh **25–45 kg**. That's a lot to lift into a shower, onto a bed or onto a hanging hook several times a week. Before you fall for a listing, ask yourself:

- Can I comfortably lift and move that weight?
- Where will she be cleaned?
- Where will she live, and can I get her there easily?

If the honest answer is "not easily", consider a lighter build, a shorter doll or a torso.

## Body types, honestly compared

### Slim builds

A slim waist, a smaller bust and leaner limbs. Typically the **lightest** full-size option, and the easiest to handle and store. Second-hand, check that the joints still hold a pose, especially on taller slim dolls, where longer limbs put more leverage on the hips and shoulders.

### Curvy and full-figured builds

A fuller bust, wider hips and thicker thighs, which many buyers specifically look for. They're **heavier**, and the extra weight means **joint wear and skin stress at the hips and knees** tend to show up sooner. A full-figured doll can be a superb buy second-hand. Just ask for close-ups of the hip and knee areas and a short posing video.

### The middle ground: often the best value

Dolls of around **155–165 cm with moderate curves** are the easiest to live with. They're substantial, but manageable to lift, clean and store. Second-hand, they're plentiful, which keeps prices competitive.

## Height and storage

- **Under 160 cm:** easier to hang in a standard wardrobe.
- **160–170 cm:** may need a taller wardrobe or a stand-alone hanging frame.
- **170 cm+:** plan your storage before you buy (see our [storage guide](/guides/how-to-store-a-doll)).

At SellMyDoll we only list full-body dolls of **150 cm or taller** with a clearly adult appearance. That's part of our [listing standards](/legal/listing-standards).

## Getting the most for your money

### Torsos: the practical entry point

If space, privacy or handling is a concern, a pre-owned **torso** is the most practical place to start. They're much lighter, far easier to clean and store, and they cost a fraction of the price of a full doll. Look for models with a removable insert or a design that's easy to clean.

### Hybrids: a premium face for less

A **hybrid**, with a hand-finished silicone head on a TPE body, gives you the realistic face of a premium doll at closer to a TPE price. Second-hand, they can be very good value. Just inspect the body as carefully as you would any TPE doll (see [TPE vs silicone](/guides/tpe-vs-silicone-pre-owned)).

### Spare heads

Already own a body? A pre-owned **spare head** is the cheapest way to change her look completely. Check the neck connector type (M16 is common) to make sure it fits.

## What to check whatever you choose

- **Material.** Does the listing clearly say TPE, S-TPE, silicone or hybrid? If not, ask.
- **The real height and weight.** Confirm them, so there are no surprises when she arrives.
- **Joints.** Ask for a short video of her being gently posed.
- **Hygiene.** Ask how she's been cleaned and stored. It's a standard question, not a rude one.
